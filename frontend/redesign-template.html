#400 – The Future of Synthetic Intelligence and Human Alignment

Executive Abstract

The conversation centers around the architectural shifts required to move from pure next-token prediction to systems capable of long-horizon planning and reliable reasoning. The guest argues that current alignment techniques (RLHF) represent a superficial patch rather than a structural solution, proposing instead a framework based on "verifiable search spaces."

Key Insight

"We are currently confusing a model's ability to imitate reasoning with the actual capacity to construct a novel proof. Imitation scales with data; proof construction scales with search compute."

Core Arguments

  • The Data Wall: High-quality human text is finite. Future gains must come from synthetic data generation filtered by objective verifiers (e.g., code execution, math proofs).
  • System 2 Thinking in LLMs: To achieve deliberate reasoning, models need an internal scratchpad that is hidden from the final output, allowing them to make mistakes, backtrack, and correct without polluting the user-facing context window.
  • Alignment Tax: Current safety training degrades base capabilities by forcing the model to collapse its probability distributions too early in the generation process.

Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization

We study the problem of reward overoptimization in reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).

As the proxy reward model is optimized, the true objective initially improves but eventually degrades. We empirically characterize this phenomenon and show that the degree of overoptimization scales predictably with the size of the policy network and the KL penalty coefficient.

Our findings suggest that current RLHF alignment techniques face a fundamental scaling limit unless verifiable ground-truth signals are introduced.
